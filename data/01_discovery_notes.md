# Meridian Catering Supplies: discovery notes

OpenKit audit, morning session at Meridian's office in Huntingdon, Thursday 1 October 2026. Rough notes, lightly tidied.

---

## Mike Deane, Owner and Managing Director

- Took over from his dad in 2015. 22 staff. Turnover around £6.8m. Growing about 10% a year, mostly through existing trade accounts ordering more.
- **What he wants:** "I want to grow without hiring two more people in the office every time we add a big account." The order desk is the bottleneck.
- **What keeps him up at night:** orders going out to customers who are on stop. "We shipped £14k of kit to a care group in the spring that went into administration. Tom had them on stop in Orbit. Nobody checked." That one hurt.
- Second worry is wrong orders. Every wrong order means a collection, restocking and a redelivery. He reckons it costs **about £85 each** in van time and labour, before the goodwill hit. They get **roughly 6 to 8 a week**.
- Not anti-tech but burned before. "We paid someone £30k in 2019 to replace Orbit and it never went live. I'm not replacing Orbit." Anything new has to sit alongside it.
- Wants to see something that works in weeks, not a big project.

## Karen Hollis, Operations Manager

- Runs the order desk, the warehouse schedule and the delivery vans.
- Shared inbox gets **60 to 90 emails a day**. About **20 to 30 of those become an order or an amendment** that has to be keyed into Orbit.
- Two people (Priya full time, and Karen herself in the afternoons) spend **about 3 hours a day each** keying orders.
- Orders come in every format: PDF purchase orders, orders typed into the email body, the odd faxed PO from care homes (their fax-to-email service turns it into a scanned PDF), and phone orders Priya writes down.
- **Biggest frustrations:**
  - Customers resend the same PO "just to check you got it" or to change a delivery date, and sometimes it gets keyed twice. Two duplicate deliveries last month.
  - Customers use their own descriptions ("the big double door fridge like last time") or old product codes.
  - "Where's my order" emails. Maybe 10 to 15 a day. Every one means looking the order up in Orbit and writing back.
- The open-items spreadsheet is "a mess but it's all we've got". Anyone adds to it. Nobody closes things properly.
- Would trust a tool that **prepares** an order for Priya to check and key. Would not trust anything that sends emails to customers on its own, "not yet anyway".

## Priya Shah, Order Processor

- Full time on the order desk. Been here 4 years.
- **Her process for a new order:**
  1. Open the email and the PDF.
  2. Find the customer in Orbit. There are a few duplicate accounts from years ago, so she checks the account notes to see which one is current.
  3. For each line, find the right Meridian product code. Customers often use old codes or their own descriptions, so she checks the price list, and for repeat items she looks up what they ordered last time in Orbit.
  4. Check the price. Each customer is on a price band (list, B or T) and some have contract deals in the account notes. If the PO price doesn't match, she flags it to Karen. "Half the time they've used an old price list."
  5. Check they're not on stop (Tom flags this in Orbit).
  6. Key it into Orbit, which takes 5 to 10 minutes per order depending on the number of lines.
  7. Reply with the acknowledgement template.
- Prices on POs are usually **ex VAT**, but some customers (the schools especially) send totals **including VAT at 20%**.
- The faxed care home POs are the worst. "Half the time I'm squinting at a smudge."
- Thinks there's a simple win in having everything she needs for an order "on one screen" before she keys it.

## Tom Reilly, Accounts and Credit Control

- Does invoicing, credit control and payments.
- Puts customers **on stop** in Orbit when they're over terms. Takes them off when they pay. "It's one field on the account screen."
- Currently on stop: **Bramley Care Group** (September invoices unpaid) and a couple of smaller ones.
- Frustrated that the order desk doesn't always check. "I've had orders go out to accounts on stop three times this year."
- Never gives verbal or email approval for someone on stop to order. "If it's lifted, it's lifted in Orbit. Full stop."
- Uses the open-items spreadsheet for invoice queries too, which is part of why it's a mess.
